How We Picked These Cards

We didn't just read marketing pages. Our methodology:

  1. Collected 40+ data points per card: earn rates, redemption values, transfer partners, fees, credits, perks, limitations.
  2. Modeled 3 spend profiles — Light ($1,500/mo), Moderate ($3,500/mo), Heavy ($6,000/mo) — across dining, travel, groceries, gas, other.
  3. Calculated net annual value = (Points earned × realistic redemption value) + Statement credits + Perk value − Annual fee.
  4. Verified transfer partners — Only counted partners with 1:1 or better ratios and award availability.
  5. Cross-referenced with Reddit/FlyerTalk sentiment and our own household usage (3 team members, 15+ cards between us).

Detailed Reviews

1. Chase Sapphire Preferred® Best Overall

Why it wins: The sweet spot of fee, flexibility, and transfer partners. 1.25¢ baseline + Hyatt/United transfers = 1.8-2.2¢ real value.

✅ 1:1 transfers to 14 partners
✅ 1.25¢ fixed cash value floor
✅ Trip cancellation/interruption insurance
❌ No lounge access
❌ 5/24 rule limits eligibility

✅ Best dining/grocery earn rate

FAQ

What's the "5/24 rule" and does it apply to me?

Chase automatically denies most applications if you've opened 5+ personal credit cards (any bank) in the last 24 months. Check your credit report — if you're at 4/24, apply for Chase cards first before adding others.

Are travel credits really "free money"?

Mostly yes, but read terms. Chase's $300 travel credit auto-applies to any travel purchase. Amex's $120 dining credit is $10/mo at specific merchants (Grubhub, Shake Shack, etc.). Capital One's $300 credit only works on Capital One Travel. Use what matches your habits.

Should I transfer points or book through the portal?

Compare both. Portal: 1.25-1.5¢ fixed, no award availability hassles. Transfers: Can hit 2-4¢/pt for business/first class or Hyatt top tiers, but require flexibility and searching. Rule of thumb: transfer for premium cabin/high-end hotels; portal for economy/last-minute.

Can I hold multiple cards from the same bank?

Yes. Chase allows multiple Sapphire cards (but only one Sapphire bonus per 48 months). Amex allows multiple Gold/Platinum. Capital One allows Venture + VentureOne. Stack strategically: e.g., CSP for travel/dining + Freedom Flex for 5x rotating categories.
